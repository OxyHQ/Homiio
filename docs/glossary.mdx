# Glossary

One definition per term, and only one. These words are used inconsistently across
the housing industry, and the inconsistency is where Homiio's hardest bugs came
from: a "property" that meant an advert to one module and a dwelling to another
produced reviews attached to listings that expired, and searches that could not
be reproduced.

Each entry says whether it exists **today**, is a **target** fixed by an ADR, or
both. Do not write code against a target as though it were current.

---

## Housing identity

### listing

**Current.** A temporary, sourced advertisement for a dwelling. A row in
`properties`.

A listing is *not* the identity of a home. It has a price, photos, availability
and an advert's text; it can expire, be withdrawn, and be republished. One
dwelling may have several listings at once across several sources, and none for
years in between.

Never use a listing id as the durable identity of a place, and never attach
anything meant to outlive the advert — a review, a rent history, an eviction
record — to one.

In the codebase the entity is spelled `Property` (`packages/shared-types/src/property.ts`)
for historical reasons. That name is not going to be re-litigated here; read it as
"listing" and note the DTO carries `isExternal`, `source`, `sourceUrl` and
`expiresAt`, which are advert properties, not building properties.

### housing place

**Target ([ADR 0001](./adr/0001-canonical-housing-graph)).** The permanent
physical thing a listing points at: a street, a building, or a unit within a
building. It is what survives every advert.

Today the closest existing thing is a row in `addresses`, which is *building
level* — see **address**. The declared `STREET` / `BUILDING` / `UNIT` levelling,
and a place-level page that exists whether or not anything is currently
advertised there, are the target and are not built.

Prefer "housing place" or "place" over "property" in prose, precisely because
"property" is ambiguous between the advert and the thing.

### building

**Target ([ADR 0001](./adr/0001-canonical-housing-graph)).** A housing place at
`BUILDING` level: one postal address, one structure. The level most public
information is published at, because it is usually coarse enough not to identify
a household.

A complex with several buildings under one postal address is explicitly handled
by ADR 0001 §3.3 — do not invent a scheme for it.

### unit

**Target ([ADR 0001](./adr/0001-canonical-housing-graph)).** A housing place at
`UNIT` level: a specific flat, door or letting within a building.

The unit is where a tenancy actually happens, so it is where a review is most
useful — and it is also where a review is most likely to identify a person.
That tension is resolved by publishing at a coarser level than you store at; see
**precision**. A review may be bound to a unit internally and published at the
building.

The `Address` interface already carries `unit`, `subunit`, `floor`, `entrance`
and `block` as building-level fields. Those fields existing is not the same as
the unit being a first-class entity, which is the part that is still target.

### address

**Current.** A row in `addresses`: a **building-level** record with street,
number, postal code and coordinates.

Two properties of it matter more than the field list:

- **Administrative geography is relational, never free text.** `country_id`,
  `region_id`, `city_id` and `neighborhood_id` are ids into the geo tables.
  `country_code` (ISO-2) is the single denormalized field, kept so a country
  filter needs no join. A city name is never copied onto an address, so renaming
  a city does not mean rewriting listings.
- **It is canonicalized on write.** `findOrCreateCanonicalAddress` computes a
  normalized key and resolves a submitted address to an existing row rather than
  creating a near-duplicate, so two spellings of one doorway do not become two
  places.

On the wire a listing carries `address` — the building-level fields, the geo ids,
**and** server-resolved display names (`cityName`, `regionName`, `countryName`,
`neighborhoodName`, and a ready-made `location` label). In storage the relation
is `properties.address_id`. Render the names; the `*Id` fields are ids.

### address candidate

**Target ([ADR 0001](./adr/0001-canonical-housing-graph) §5.3).** A proposed
address that has not been accepted into the canonical set — a geocoder
suggestion, a user-typed string, or an ingest result that did not match an
existing row confidently.

The point of the concept is that a candidate must be *resolvable or rejectable*
rather than silently promoted. An unresolved candidate becoming an address is how
one dwelling ends up as three places with one review each.

Not implemented. `address_candidates` does not exist yet; today an ingest either
matches a canonical address or creates one.

### place

**Ambiguous — say which you mean.** Two distinct uses, and conflating them is a
real source of confusion:

1. **A housing place** — the physical dwelling above.
2. **A geographic place** — a country, region, city or neighborhood: a row in
   the geo tables, used to scope a search. This is the sense in
   [ADR 0002](./adr/0002-location-and-search-contract) and in
   `location.kind: 'city' | 'neighborhood' | 'region' | 'country'`.

There is a third, unrelated: `place_pois` caches OpenStreetMap points of interest
for the "nearby services" section. It stores presence and counts by category and
deliberately holds **no place names** — the section answers "is there a pharmacy
nearby?", never "which pharmacy?".

## Trust and provenance

### review

**Current.** A resident's account of living somewhere, attached to an **address**
rather than to a listing — so it outlives the advert that led someone there.

Moderation is community-level and there is no privileged actor: reports
accumulate on a review until it moves to `under_review` automatically. Homiio has
no admin panel and no moderator queue, by explicit product decision.

Agency reviews are a separate surface with the same shape, attached to an
`agencies` row.

**Target ([ADR 0003](./adr/0003-privacy-verification-publication) §5):** author
identity in three chosen forms, right of reply, corrections distinguished from
edits, immutable version history, and the rule that a review bound to a unit
internally may be published at the building. Also **target
([ADR 0001](./adr/0001-canonical-housing-graph) §4.2):** the write / read /
aggregate hierarchy across street, building and unit.

### verification

**Partly current.** A claim that has been checked, as distinct from a claim that
has been made. `isVerified` exists on a listing today.

**Target ([ADR 0003](./adr/0003-privacy-verification-publication) §6):** graded
verification levels, residence evidence handled by extracting the signal and
discarding the document, and one rule that keeps being got wrong —
**verification is not endorsement.** A verified resident's review is not thereby
true, and the UI must not imply that it is.

### precision

**Target ([ADR 0003](./adr/0003-privacy-verification-publication) §3), and the
single most important term here.**

A value is stored at one precision and published at another. The reduction
happens **on the way out**, in the serializer, from a viewer plus a precision —
never by storing a second, coarser copy of the entity, and never by each caller
deciding for itself.

`ADDRESS_PRECISION_LEVELS` in the observability schema already names the ladder:
`unit`, `building`, `street`, `locality`, `area`.

Two current instances: eviction cases publish deliberately approximate
coordinates, and observability events carry bucketed bands (radius, area, zoom,
result count, text length) rather than values. Widening a published precision
anywhere is an ADR 0003 decision, not a code review one.

### source

**Current.** Where an external listing came from. Three fields together:
`source` (the portal, e.g. `idealista`), `sourceId` (the portal's own id), and
`sourceUrl` (the mandatory link back). The upsert key is `(source, sourceId)`.

Source is **provenance shown to the user**, not an internal detail: an external
listing is visibly marked, cannot be applied to or booked in-app, and links back
to the portal. Direct contact details are shown only when the portal itself
exposed them — never inferred, never guessed.

Do not confuse `source` with a **provider**, which is the plugin in
`@homiio/listing-providers` that fetched it. One provider can produce listings
for several regional sources.

**Target ([ADR 0004](./adr/0004-local-explainable-pricing)):** provenance
extended with age and confidence, so a stale price is visibly stale.

### snapshot

**Target ([ADR 0004](./adr/0004-local-explainable-pricing), #367).** An immutable
record of what a listing said at a moment: price, deposit, fees, conditions and
provenance, kept as a series so a dwelling has a price *history* rather than only
a current price.

The distinction from the current state is exact: `priceEthics` on a listing today
is a single overwritten field with a `scoredAt` timestamp. Overwriting is the
opposite of a snapshot — it destroys the history the feature needs.

Not implemented. Nothing today records what a listing used to cost.

## Related terms you will meet in the code

| Term | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| **offering** | How a listing is offered: `long_term_rent`, `short_term_rent`, `sale`, `exchange`. `offerings` is an array and must exactly equal the set of priced blocks present. |
| **priced block** | The pricing object backing one offering: `longTermRent`, `shortTermRent`, `sale`, `exchange`. Units are fixed — monthly is always monthly, nightly always nightly. |
| **profile** | Optional Homiio-side real-estate metadata keyed by `oxyUserId`. A sidecar, never an identity or an ownership authority. |
| **oxyUserId** | The identity, from the Oxy session. The owner key on every write. |
| **external listing** | `isExternal: true`, no `oxyUserId`, mandatory `sourceUrl`, expires. In-app apply and viewing are blocked for it. |
| **expiry sweep** | The registered job that deletes expired rows. Postgres does not do this by itself; see `packages/backend/db/expiry.ts`. |
| **wire id** | Every identity on the wire is `id`. See [Architecture](./architecture) for the serializer that guarantees it. |
| **`LocationSelection`** | **Target** ([ADR 0002](./adr/0002-location-and-search-contract)): the one shape every surface uses to say "where", carried in state, the URL and the API. |
| **housing place page** | **Target** (#362): a page for a dwelling that exists whether or not anything is listed there. |

## Naming rules

- **Say "listing" when you mean the advert and "place" when you mean the
  dwelling.** "Property" means both and is therefore the wrong word in prose,
  even though it is the type name.
- **A place name is never stored as text on an entity.** It is an id into the geo
  tables, resolved for display on the way out.
- **Do not introduce a synonym.** If a concept here needs a new name, it probably
  needs an ADR instead.
